<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>420 people on a mountain, and what stayed with me</strong></h3>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Last week we brought the QGIS User Conference up to 2222 metres. <strong>Four hundred and twenty people from more than thirty countries and five continents</strong> got into a gondola in Laax and came up to <em>Crap Sogn Gion</em>, and for two days the <em>Galaaxy</em> was full of talks, six keynotes, twelve short workshops and a lot of coffee. It is the biggest conference that has ever happened on this mountain. A week later I am still a little bit amazed that it worked.</p>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But the logistics are not what stayed with me.</p>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We did not put digital sovereignty on the programme as a track, and still it was everywhere. <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/gerhard-andrey-aa57a820/">Gerhard Andrey</a> opened on Monday morning with Switzerland and open source, and from there it simply did not go away. For the people from public administrations in the room this is not philosophy. It is a line in a budget. They have data they must keep readable for thirty years, and tools whose conditions can change from one year to the next.</p>
<div class="wp-block-image">
<figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="Gerhard Andrey Keynote on Digital sovereignty" class="wp-image-18656" height="563" src="/img/subscribers/opengisch/qgis-user-conference-2026-in-laax-%f0%9f%8f%94%ef%b8%8f%f0%9f%97%ba%ef%b8%8f%f0%9f%92%9a/1791837857116.webp" style="width: 461px; height: auto;" width="750"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Gerhard Andrey Keynote on Digital sovereignty</figcaption></figure>

<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The conversation I keep thinking about was with a man who used to sell proprietary GIS licences for a living. At some point he did the maths on what his country, which is not a rich country, pays every year for software it will never own, and he decided the money would do more good building something his country actually controls. So he quit. Now he works on open national geospatial infrastructure. He did not tell me this like a big story. He told it like you tell someone you changed job.</p>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That one <strong>stayed with me</strong> for the rest of the week.</p>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/lorenz-hurni-bb16a926a/">Lorenz Hurni</a> came after Andrey with <strong>maps as instruments of power</strong>, which is a good reminder for anyone who still thinks a basemap is something neutral. <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/anita-graser-%F0%9F%8C%BB-95102530/">Anita Graser <img alt="🌻" class="wp-smiley" src="/img/subscribers/opengisch/qgis-user-conference-2026-in-laax-%f0%9f%8f%94%ef%b8%8f%f0%9f%97%ba%ef%b8%8f%f0%9f%92%9a/1f33b.webp" style="height: 1em;"/></a> talked about <strong>AI in the QGIS ecosystem</strong>, <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/johannes-kanonier-982843336/">Johannes Kanonier</a> took us through fifteen years of QGIS at the <strong>State of Vorarlberg</strong>, and <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/ivan-min%C4%8D%C3%ADk-937647264/">Ivan Minčík</a>, <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/xpirix/">Lova Andriarimalala</a> and <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/tim-sutton-8038661b/">Tim Sutton</a> made the least glamorous and most necessary point of the week, which is that <strong>somebody has to</strong> <strong>maintain the infrastructure</strong> <strong>and that somebody needs to be paid.</strong></p>
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Here is the thing I keep coming back to. Open source does not give you sovereignty by itself. If you download free software and then nobody in your country can fix it, extend it or answer for it, you have only changed the shape of your dependency. <strong>What makes it real</strong> is what happened in those corridors, where the people who pay for the software and the people who write it were standing in the same line waiting for lunch.</p>
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity"/>
<div class="wp-block-image">
<figure class="aligncenter size-large is-resized"><img alt="" class="wp-image-18664" height="500" src="/img/subscribers/opengisch/qgis-user-conference-2026-in-laax-%f0%9f%8f%94%ef%b8%8f%f0%9f%97%ba%ef%b8%8f%f0%9f%92%9a/1791837986379.webp" style="width: 639px; height: auto;" width="750"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Discussing and taking</figcaption></figure>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And that is not a nice image, it is literally what happened. On Wednesday we ran <strong>ten full-day workshops</strong> down in the valley with 120 people, on point clouds, data processing, plugin development, automation, collaboration and field data infrastructure. Seventy-one people spent the <strong>QField Day</strong> walking around Laax in small groups collecting data. From Wednesday to Friday the<strong> 29th Contributor Meeting</strong> put developers, translators and documentation people around the same tables, and some of what was discussed on Monday was already a commit by Thursday. <strong>Three days from a problem on somebody’s desk to a fix in the code.</strong> That loop is the real product.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And around all of that there was <strong>the other programme</strong>, the one that never appears as a session in any schedule. On Sunday, before the conference even started, ninety people <strong>walked</strong> the <em>Ruinaulta and the Segnes</em> <em>plateau</em> with guides. On Monday and Tuesday whoever took the first gondolas could do <strong>yoga</strong> at eight in the morning on a terrace at 2222 metres, or a short walk, or a <strong>QField crash course</strong>, or a crash course in <strong>Romansh</strong>. There were <strong>trail runs</strong> on Wednesday and Friday for the people who consider six or ten kilometres before breakfast a reasonable idea. And on Tuesday, when the conference was over, a few of us did not take the gondola back down. <strong>We flew.</strong> Launching off Crap Sogn Gion next to <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/regishaubourg/">Régis Haubourg</a> from the PSC was how a dream that had come true finally closed.</p>
<p class="wp-block-paragraph">I am fairly sure more <strong>friendships</strong> came out of those hours than out of any session.</p>
